# Imagery and Content Voice

Photography and copy carry most of the brand's emotion. Extract their rules with the same rigour as colour and type.

## Imagery

### Source policy

Record:

- client-supplied versus licensed stock;
- remote versus self-hosted;
- image manifest and processing pipeline;
- maximum dimensions, format, quality, and total weight;
- EXIF and GPS stripping;
- credit and licence status.

Do not download, bundle, or redistribute client photography in the generated skill. Reference slots, not assets.

### Art direction

Record:

- light: golden hour, low sun, overcast, studio, night;
- subject: landscapes, people, vehicles, interiors, products, animals;
- treatment: natural, warm, desaturated, high contrast, black and white;
- crop: wide, portrait, square, panoramic;
- focal points;
- whether people are participants or models;
- whether generic images are used for specific places.

### Composition

- full-bleed versus contained;
- scrim only when text sits on the image;
- text placement and contrast;
- image ratios by component;
- hover behaviour: reveal copy, not zoom, unless zoom is the established pattern;
- placeholder treatment.

### Placeholders

A designed placeholder is part of the system. Record:

- tone or seed palette;
- label;
- ratio;
- alt text fallback;
- whether the placeholder is obviously temporary.

A broken image or a borrowed park photo is a trust failure.

### Alt text

- decorative images: empty alt;
- meaningful images: specific, factual alt;
- text in images: repeat the text in copy;
- placeholders: say the image is pending;
- do not keyword-stuff.

### Credits

Record every credit and licence. `TODO confirm credit` is a development placeholder, not a launch-ready credit. Flag unresolved credits as P0.

## Content voice

### Voice attributes

Record three to five attributes with evidence:

- quiet or loud;
- expert or playful;
- sparse or verbose;
- formal or conversational;
- hedged or certain;
- local or global.

### Sentence patterns

Collect representative examples:

- eyebrow labels;
- headlines;
- ledes;
- section intros;
- card excerpts;
- CTA labels;
- trust statements;
- error and empty states;
- legal and pricing language.

### Rules

- Prefer concrete nouns and place names.
- Use short declarative sentences.
- State numerals and durations plainly.
- Use honest hedges when the facts are uncertain.
- Use verbs for calls to action.
- Avoid generic "Welcome to" copy.
- Avoid emoji in UI copy unless the brand explicitly uses it.
- Do not invent reviews, awards, certifications, scarcity, or guarantees.

### Trust mechanics

Record what the site uses instead of generic badges:

- real address, phone, email;
- live counts from a database;
- client-supplied photography;
- specific itinerary detail;
- cautious availability language;
- "or similar" accommodation language;
- explicit "pricing is quoted per trip";
- "photo pending" and "schematic route" honesty.

### Conversion copy

Record:

- primary CTA;
- secondary CTA;
- tertiary contact path;
- form labels and hints;
- success and error messages;
- privacy and consent language.

A premium brand does not need urgency. It needs clarity.

## Content inventory

For each page, list:

- headline;
- lede;
- section headings;
- body copy blocks;
- card titles and excerpts;
- metadata;
- CTAs;
- proof;
- legal and pricing notes.

Mark missing content as `content needed`, not as an invitation to invent.

## Anti-patterns

- Stock photography that implies a place the client does not serve.
- Isolated animal cutouts on a safari site that uses documentary photography.
- Decorative gradients over photography.
- Fake reviews, awards, ratings, or "as seen in" logos.
- Generic SaaS copy in an editorial brand.
- Keyword-stuffed alt text.
- Placeholder lorem ipsum in a launch audit.
- Unresolved credits.
